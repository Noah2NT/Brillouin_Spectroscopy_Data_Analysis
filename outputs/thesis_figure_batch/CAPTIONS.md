# Figure captions

## Review figure 1

Mechanical differences can be measured despite similar appearances. (a) An AFM probe presses into a cell. (b) At the same applied force F, the higher-stiffness response has less indentation, delta_H < delta_L. The curves illustrate the contact response.

## Review figure 2

Brillouin microscopy produces a spectrum at each scan position. (a) Incident light at f_inc is focused into the sample and backscattered light is directed to a separate spectrometer and detector. Acoustic-wave motifs indicate fluctuations within the sample. (b) Repeating the measurement produces a spatial scan. (c) Expanding guides connect one pixel to its spectrum. The incident-light, Stokes and anti-Stokes peaks occur at shifts 0, -f_B and +f_B.

## Review figure 3

Reading a Brillouin spectrum. (a) BFS is the centre-to-centre separation from the incident-light peak to a Brillouin peak; FWHM, Gamma, is the width at half peak height. All drawn peaks are Lorentzian. (b) A change in peak centre shifts BFS. (c) Increased linewidth broadens the peak at constant integrated intensity.

## Review figure 4

Schematic separated Brillouin responses illustrate how material identity can produce different BFS values. Water and PMMA are introductory examples, not the glycerol simulation calibration. Peak centres and widths are illustrative.

## Review figure 5

A finite collection region can contain both sample and surrounding medium. Their collection-weighted local spectra combine into one measured spectrum. Panels (b) and (c) use the same axes and contributions; the example is schematic, not a depth reconstruction.

## Review figure 6

Fewer detected photons produce greater relative shot noise. Connected orange traces are Poisson realisations and grey traces are their noiseless expectations for the same fixed mixture. The pure-water reference budgets P are 100000, 10000 and 1000 expected photons in the retained 4.50–8.50 GHz band. All 401 bin centres are spaced by 0.01 GHz; endpoint bins are clipped to the retained band. Raw photon-count scales vary between panels.

## Review figure 7

A single Lorentzian cannot exactly reproduce this deliberately chosen two-component sum. The plotted single-peak curve is a numerical least-squares fit to the same noiseless sum shown in panel (a). This example illustrates model mismatch, not a universal claim that every mixture is visibly separable. Closely overlapping components can also produce deceptively small residuals.

## Review figure 8

Known and unknown component spectra lead to different recovery tasks. (a) Supplied reference spectra are fitted to estimate their normalised fractions. (b) Multiple mixed spectra provide variation for estimating unknown component spectra and their fractions. The displayed spectra and fractions illustrate the tasks rather than fitted benchmark results.

## Review figure 10

Three-dimensional prescribed sample models in surrounding water. The uniform sphere has 10% glycerol by mass; the cutaway gradient sphere increases from 0% at the boundary to 10% at the centre. The red cylinder array illustrates collection volumes at multiple lateral scan positions, each with the finite axial collection interval (30 micrometres, from z=0 to 30). The cutaway reveals a radial model field, not a reconstructed volume. Concentration laws are test functions, not physical diffusion models; dimensions are in micrometres.

## Review figure 11

A single axial collection region contains water and homogeneous 10% glycerol solution. The central ray has collection fractions 1/3 water and 2/3 solution (33.3% and 66.7%); their full-band signal fractions are 29.4% and 70.6%. The coloured curves already include those weights and the chosen concentration-dependent areas; their exact sum is the black curve. These coefficients are collection fractions, not glycerol mass fractions or necessarily equal signal fractions. Spectra include the assumed instrument response.

## Review figure 12

A varying sample produces different local spectra within one collection volume. (a) Coloured points mark depth positions in the varying sample. (b) Local spectra are individually scaled and vertically offset to show their shapes. (c) Depth-weighted concentration-bin and water contributions combine into the black spectrum. The colour bar gives glycerol concentration as percent by mass.

## Review figure 13

Chosen local response laws over the initial 0–10% glycerol mass range. BFS and intrinsic linewidth use the provisional laws specified in the methodology. Integrated intensity is prescribed to rise linearly by 20% between 0% and 10% glycerol. This increasing intensity is an explicit modelling choice, not a measured concentration calibration. Peak area and peak height are distinct.

The display window is 5.65–7.00 GHz, while the retained band is 4.50–8.50 GHz. Both rows share frequency and intensity scales and include instrumental broadening.
