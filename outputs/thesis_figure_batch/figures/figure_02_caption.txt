Principle of Brillouin microscopy and spectral image formation. (a) Incident laser light at frequency f_inc is focused into the sample through an objective. The same objective collects backscattered light, which is directed through a beam splitter to a separate spectrometer and detector. Backscattering is labelled theta = 180 degrees. Vertical wave motifs symbolise longitudinal acoustic fluctuations along the optical axis; they are not literal trajectories of material particles. (b) Repeating the acquisition across x and y produces a spatial grid containing a spectrum at each position; the orange square identifies one position. (c) Expanding dashed guides link that pixel to its schematic spectrum, showing the central elastic Rayleigh peak and frequency-shifted Stokes and anti-Stokes peaks. Frequency shifts are relative to the laser. Optical paths, wave amplitudes and peak shapes are schematic and not to scale; the spectrum does not represent measured or simulated quantitative data.
